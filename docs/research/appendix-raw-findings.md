

# ANGLE: tax

Research covered UK tax and pension parameters for tax year 2026/27 (6 April 2026 – 5 April 2027) plus announced future changes from Autumn Budget 2025 (26 Nov 2025) and the Spring Statement/Forecast (3 Mar 2026). 19 searches were run and roughly 35 pages opened; nearly all figures below are read directly from gov.uk, gov.scot, gov.wales, HMRC manuals (PTM), DWP's rates document, legislation.gov.uk or the parliamentary record.

Income tax (England/Wales/NI): personal allowance £12,570, tapered £1 per £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000 (nil at £125,140); basic 20% on £12,571–£50,270 (band width £37,700), higher 40% to £125,140, additional 45% above. These thresholds are legislated to stay frozen to 5 April 2031. Wales sets 10p in each band, so rates match England/NI. Scotland 2026/27 has six bands: 19% to £16,537, 20% to £29,526, 21% to £43,662, 42% to £75,000, 45% to £125,140, 48% above (basic/intermediate thresholds raised 7.4%, upper thresholds frozen).

Savings/dividends/CGT: PSA £1,000/£500/£0 and £5,000 starting rate unchanged; savings-income rates rise to 22/42/47% from 6 April 2027. Dividend allowance £500; dividend rates 10.75%/35.75%/39.35% from 6 April 2026 (property income rates also go to 22/42/47% from April 2027). CGT: AEA £3,000 (£1,500 trusts); 18% within the basic rate band, 24% above; BADR 18% from April 2026; carried interest moves into income tax from April 2026.

NI Class 1 (employee): LEL £129/wk (£6,708), PT £242/wk (£12,570), UEL £967/wk (£50,270); 8% between PT and UEL, 2% above. Employer 15% above £5,000 secondary threshold; Employment Allowance £10,500.

Pensions: annual allowance £60,000; MPAA £10,000; taper when threshold income > £200,000 and adjusted income > £260,000, £1 per £2, minimum £10,000 (reached at £360,000). Carry forward: three previous years, must have been a scheme member in the year carried from, current year used first then earliest year, not available against the MPAA, no HMRC notification needed. Relievable contributions capped at the greater of 100% relevant UK earnings or £3,600 gross (£2,880 net). LSA £268,275; LSDBA £1,073,100; tax-free cash normally 25% subject to those allowances. UFPLS: 25% tax-free, 75% taxed as pension income; HMRC instructs schemes to tax first flexible payments using the emergency code on a week 1/month 1 basis, with over-deductions reclaimed via P55 (pot not emptied), P53Z (pot emptied, other income) or P50Z (pot emptied, stopped working). Emergency code for 2026/27 is 1257L (likely; PDF not text-readable). Relief at source adds 20%; higher/additional rate taxpayers claim a further 20%/25% (Scottish 1%–28%) via Self Assessment; net pay deducts from gross pay, and low earners in net pay schemes get an HMRC top-up from 2024/25. NMPA is 55, rising to 57 on 6 April 2028 (uniformed services and protected pension ages exempt).

State pension 2026/27: full new SP £241.30/wk (from £230.25), basic Cat A/B £184.90/wk (from £176.45), both +4.8% under the triple lock (highest of earnings growth, CPI or 2.5%; earnings May–Jul 2025 won). Pension Credit SMG £238.00 single/£363.25 couple. SPA 66→67 phased for those born 6 April 1960–5 March 1961 (6 May 2026–6 March 2028), 67 for births to 5 April 1977, 68 phased 2044–46 (under review).

ISAs: £20,000 overall; LISA £4,000 (25% bonus, max £1,000/yr, open before 40, pay to 50, 25% withdrawal charge, £450,000 cap); JISA £9,000. From 6 April 2027 the cash ISA limit is £12,000 for under-65s (65+ keep £20,000), with a 22% flat charge on interest on cash inside non-cash ISAs and no transfers from non-cash ISAs into cash ISAs. LISA to be replaced by a First Time Buyer ISA (consultation closes 18 Aug 2026, no start date). IHT: unused pension funds and most death benefits enter the estate for deaths on or after 6 April 2027 (Finance Act 2026, RA 18 Mar 2026); PRs liable; 50% withholding up to 15 months; death-in-service, dependants' scheme pensions, charity lump sums excluded. Salary sacrifice: employer and employee NICs on salary-sacrificed employer pension contributions above £2,000/yr from 6 April 2029 (NICs (Employer Pensions Contributions) Act 2026, RA 29 Apr 2026); income tax treatment unchanged. Spring Forecast 2026 announced no new tax/pension measures.

# ANGLE: integrations

Selectapension sits inside a small, well-defined UK adviser-tech data ecosystem. Its confirmed integrations (own site + partner pages + press) are: back-office/CRMs Intelliflo Office (iO Store app since Sept 2018), Iress Xplan (Iress Open "standard integration", enabled free by the firm's Xplan admin), Plannr (Apr 2024, cashflow), True Potential (listed, no technical detail public), Iress Adviser Office (Paradigm page), 4admin (Feb 2026); data suppliers Morningstar (full feed: 70,000+ funds, ~90 DFM MPS providers, performance/charges/product availability, plus MPS performance since Nov 2024), Assureweb/iPipeline (live annuity quotes using the adviser's own Assureweb credentials) and AKG (with-profits); and Origo Integration Hub (OIH), which underpins its "Quote & Apply" account-opening/pre-population flow (Aegon first, Nov 2020). Time4Advice, Advisory AI, ZeroKey and Finplan are "coming soon". No evidence was found of FE fundinfo or Defaqto data being used by Selectapension.

Origo Integration Hub (Origo Services Ltd, Edinburgh) is a hub-and-spoke exchange offering six services: Account Opening & trading instructions, Contract Enquiry (single) valuations, Bulk Valuations, Bulk Transaction History, Remuneration and Transfer Tracking. It reached 42 orgs (Selectapension named) in 2021, 100+ live connections in 2023 and 65+ orgs in 2026 (True Potential joined Aug 2026). The public SS onboarding guides are the key technical source: single valuations are synchronous HTTP POSTs of Criterion-standard XML (Contract Enquiry Request; Pension/Bond/CIV/Wrap standards, MTG 2.1 headers) to https://oih.origoservices.com/api/getValuation (UAT: oihuat...), authenticated by the software supplier's Organisational Unipass certificate, with the adviser's Unipass Identity X509 data embedded so the provider can authorise the adviser as Servicing Agent; 30-second timeout; OIH00nnn error codes. Bulk valuations/transactions/remuneration are daily CSV/XML files pulled over SFTP (oih-sftp.origoservices.com) with <tpsdata> extension blocks. Onboarding is: initial call, UAT config, stub testing, end-to-end testing, both-party go-live approval, commercial contract. Criterion standards are now independent (criterion.org.uk), including a Platform Account Opening standard.

Intelliflo's public API is OAuth2/OIDC (identity.gb.intelliflo.net/core/connect; authorization-code for user consent, "tenant client credentials" for server-to-server with tenant_id) plus a mandatory x-api-key header, base https://api.gb.intelliflo.net with /v2 paths: clients, clients/{id}/plans (plan = product/policy, latest valuation embedded), holdings timeseries, assets valuations, documents, valuations/batches (bulk valuation import with plan matching headers), funds/prices/providers, advisers and WebSub webhooks. Scopes observed include client_data, client_financial_data, firm_data, fund_data, valuation_batch, hub, apps, offline_access. Apps are distributed via the intelliflo store after certification; docs live at developer.gb.intelliflo.net (JS SPA, login needed for detail). 250+ endpoints, 80+ store apps.

Iress Open Standard API is a productised subset of the Xplan API (Swagger at api.iressopen.co.uk, v3 current, v2 deprecated; UAT api.uat.iressopen.co.uk). Third parties need an API key, the firm's Xplan site URL, Xplan credentials and developer-community access; OAuth2 supported; the end-user grants permission via an Xplan login redirect. Resources include client Address/Asset etc.; documents are posted back as client Notes.

Morningstar's developer offering is Direct Web Services: JWT bearer from POST /token/oauth (Basic auth, 60 min), regional hosts (us-api/emea-api), investments, investment-details, screener, time-series and portfolio-analysis endpoints; credentials/licensing via a Morningstar account manager. The Security Details datapoint catalogue covers identifiers (ISIN/SEDOL), KIID ongoing charge and MiFID cost data, Morningstar Category, Morningstar Rating (overall/3/5/10y), Medalist rating, trailing/calendar/rolling returns, alpha/beta, asset allocation, region/sector, KIID/factsheet documents and sustainability scores. Morningstar also has a UK Managed Portfolio Database (~900 portfolios, fees and total cost). Alternatives: FE fundinfo (100k+ funds, REST API/feeds/files, Crown ratings) and Defaqto (21k+ funds/platforms/MPS, Diamond ratings, daily-refreshed API/feeds, existing iO/Xplan/Plannr integrations).

# ANGLE: regulation

The FCA rules that a Selectapension-style tool must encode fall into four calculation regimes plus the advice-process rules that wrap them.

DB transfers (COBS 19.1, in force from 1 Oct 2018 via PS18/6, amended by PS20/6 from 1 Oct 2020). Before any personal recommendation the firm must run an Appropriate Pension Transfer Analysis (APTA, COBS 19.1.2BR + Annex 4A/4C) and produce a Transfer Value Comparator (TVC, COBS 19.1.3AR + Annex 4B/4C/5). The TVC is fully prescribed: revalue the deferred DB pension to NRA (RPI 3.0%, CPI/LPI-CPI 2.0%, LPI-RPI 3.0%, s148/AEI 3.5%), price a pension annuity at NRA using the 3-month average of the COBS 13 Annex 2 3.1R(6) intermediate annuity rate (CPI-linked = RPI rate +1.0%), PMA16/PFA16 with CMI (20YY-2)_M/F_[1.25%] improvements, 4.0% annuity expense allowance, spouse assumed 3 years younger (male member)/older (female member), then discount to today at the term-appropriate FTSE Actuaries fixed-coupon gilt yield (bands ≤5, 5-10, 10-15, >15 yrs, refreshed 6th of each month from the 15th of the prior month) net of a 0.4% p.a. product charge (cut from 0.75% by PS20/6). Output must use the Annex 5 bar chart and wording ("It could cost you £X to obtain a comparable level of income from an insurer"), y-axis from £0, with the three prescribed notes. APTA itself uses adviser-chosen growth rates that reflect the proposed investments, must include all charges (product, platform, adviser, decumulation), model beyond average life expectancy, compare against the workplace default, and any cashflow model must be in real terms at the 2.0% CPI assumption with stress tests. Stochastic alternatives are allowed only if the 50th percentile is no less conservative (19.1.2CR). Critical yield was the pre-2018 TVAS metric (CP17/16 4.1: "the rate of return … necessary to reproduce the safeguarded benefits being given up, assuming the purchase of an annuity"); it is no longer required and FG21/3 flags TVAS-style multiple critical yields as poor practice. Contingent charging is banned (COBS 19.1B) except for serious ill-health/serious financial difficulty; abridged advice (COBS 19.1A) may not include APTA/TVC.

Projections/KFIs (COBS 13 Annex 2/3/4). Standardised deterministic projections use lower/intermediate/higher rates 2%/5%/8% nominal maximum for pensions and tax-exempt wrappers, 1.5%/4.5%/7.5% for other products, with a mandatory 3% spread and the intermediate rate capped at what the fund can realistically earn. Pension projections must be shown in real terms using intermediate price inflation, currently 2.00% (RPI-linked items 3.00%; earnings ≥3.5%), rounded down to 3 significant figures. Reduction in yield is defined algebraically: RIY = intermediate rate B minus the rate C (to nearest 0.1%) that would reproduce the charged projection value with charges removed; personal pensions must show a product RIY and a total RIY including adviser charges, plus an "effect of charges" table (before charges / product charges only / all charges at years 1,3,5 and retirement). Future annuity projections use Y = 0.5*(ILG0+ILG5)-0.5 (rounded to 0.2%), level annuities Y+1.5/3.5/5.5%, RPI/LPI Y-1/Y/Y+1, 4% expenses, PMA16/PFA16, 5-year guarantee.

Pension switching (no safeguarded benefits). No prescribed calculation; suitability under COBS 9 plus COBS 19.2.2R (explain why at least as suitable as a stakeholder pension and as any workplace scheme). The FSA's Dec 2008 thematic review (30 firms, 500 files, 16% unsuitable; 79% of unsuitable cases involved extra cost without good reason) and its Feb 2009 template treat "more expensive than ceding scheme or a stakeholder pension without good reason" as a key unsuitable outcome and capture RIY of ceding vs receiving scheme, penalties, guarantees/GARs, ATR match and ongoing reviews.

Suitability reports (COBS 9.4.7R / 9A.3.2-3R) must state demands and needs, why suitable and any disadvantages; DB transfer reports need a one-page summary with cash-terms charges vs ceding scheme and workplace default, and payback months of the initial fee from revalued monthly DB income. Consumer Duty PRIN 2A.4 (from 31 Jul 2023) requires fair-value assessments that consider the expected total price over the product lifetime including AMCs and contingent charges. SMPIs follow AS TM1 v5.2 (from 6 Apr 2026): accumulation rates by 5-year monthly-return volatility group 2%/4%/6%/7% (bands <5, 5-10, 10-15, ≥15%), inflation and earnings 2.5%, single-life level annuity from the 15-year gilt yield with 4% expenses. UK PRIIPs KIDs used an RIY summary cost indicator (IRR with vs without costs, Annex VI para 70-72); the CCI/DISC product summary replacing it (optional 6 Apr 2026, mandatory 8 Jun 2027) drops it for a headline ongoing costs figure.

## Open questions
- COBS 13 Annex 2 2.5R currently shows intermediate price inflation 2.00% (with RPI-linked items 3.00%) and all Annex 2 rules carry a 19/11/2025 update date; I could not retrieve the pre-November-2025 text (widely cited as RPI 2.5%) because the Handbook's date-view requires JavaScript, so confirm the effective date of the 2.0%/3.0% split before hard-coding it for historic illustrations.
- The numeric value of 'Y' (COBS 13 Annex 2 3.1R(6)) and the current TVC gilt yields/annuity rates are not published as figures on the Handbook; they must be derived each year/month from FTSE Actuaries index-linked and fixed-coupon indices (15 Feb for Y; 15th of prior month for TVC yields). A data feed or licensed index values are needed.
- The primary FSA December 2008 report 'Quality of advice on pension switching' could not be opened (pfca.org.uk mirror has a broken certificate; National Archives snapshots are JS-rendered). The 30 firms/500 files/16% and 79/40/26/14% statistics are from M&G and Royal London secondary sources plus search summaries and are marked 'likely'.
- COBS 19 Annex 5 Table 1/2 prescribed TVC wording was read from the Handbook's PNG graphics (api-handbook.fca.org.uk); exact typography and any 2026 amendments should be re-checked against the live page before templating.
- AS TM1 v5.1 (6 April 2024) raised rates from v5.0's 1/3/5/7% to the 2/4/6/7% now in v5.2; I confirmed v5.0 and v5.2 from the FRC PDFs but did not open the v5.1 PDF itself (URL: https://www.frc.org.uk/documents/6987/AS_TM1_-_Statutory_Money_Purchase_Illustrations_Version_5.1.pdf).
- There is no FCA rule defining 'critical yield' for DC-to-DC pension switching; the FSA template only captures RIY and charges, so any critical-yield-to-match-existing-plan calculation in a switching tool is an industry convention, not a Handbook requirement.
- PS22/9/FG22/5 contain no advice-firm-specific fair value numbers; the Consumer Duty implications for a charges-comparison tool are inferential (lifetime total price incl. AMC and contingent charges per PRIN 2A.4.8R).


# ANGLE: competitors

The UK adviser-tech market is fragmented into six overlapping layers, and no vendor spans all of them: (1) switching/DB analysis engines: O&M Profiler ESP (Iress, since March 2020), Selectapension, Defaqto Engage's pension and product/platform switching modules, FE fundinfo's ex-AdviserAsset "Pension Switching & Platform Due Diligence" tools and Synaptic's ex-ante RIY comparison; (2) cashflow modellers: Voyant, FE CashCalc, Timeline, Dynamic Planner Cash Flow, intelliflo planning (ex-i4C, now bundled free into intelliflo office, ~46% adviser share) and Prestwood Truth; (3) risk profilers: Dynamic Planner (most used, 23-25%), Defaqto (19%), Synaptic (Moody's stochastic engine), FE (EValue), Timeline; (4) research: FE Analytics (300k instruments), Defaqto (21k funds/products), Synaptic (140k funds); (5) client engagement: Money Alive video box sets, moneyinfo (ex-Sammedia) portals; (6) CRM: Plannr (£140/month, 25% Fintel-owned), intelliflo, Xplan. I found no Fundment ownership of Plannr, only an account-opening integration.

Calculation coverage: O&M and Selectapension are the only tools doing the full switching stack (critical yield, RIY, projected values, TVC/APTA with single/joint-life critical yields, hurdle rates, PPF, death-benefit comparison, drawdown vs annuity three-way comparisons, Assureweb annuity quotes). Defaqto/FE/Synaptic do RIY/total-cost comparisons but not critical yield or TVC. Stochastic cashflow: Dynamic Planner (own model, 5th/50th/95th), Defaqto (Hymans Robertson), Synaptic (Moody's ESG), CashCalc and Voyant (Monte Carlo), Truth (probability calculator); Timeline uses rolling historical periods; intelliflo's stochastic capability is unconfirmed.

Public pricing (ex VAT unless stated): O&M ESP £45/£75/£120 per month for 1/2/3 modules + £20 extra user + £100 setup; Selectapension bureau from £225 per report; Defaqto switching module £30/user/month (base licence not public); Synaptic Pathways £175/user/month enterprise (£25-£35 basics); FE CashCalc £75/month (brochure Dec 2023 said £60); FE Analytics ~£175/month inc VAT (2022 comment); Voyant AdviserGo £175/seat/month + ClientGo £175 per 25 clients; Timeline £142/month unlimited clients; Dynamic Planner £60 profiling / £124 review / £179 review plus + £130 setup + £8/client/yr app; Money Alive £99/month starter; Truth £197/£297 (2014).
